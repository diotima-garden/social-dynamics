# Calibration — Dating Frame-Control

The gold-standard set the grader is anchored to. `../../improvement-plan.md` #1.

**What this is for.** The grader *is* the feedback mechanism, and since the simulator
started asserting worked answers it also decides what right looks like. Its validity is
otherwise unestablished. This file is the check: a small set of cases where the correct
grade is known, chosen because they are the cases this grader is known to get wrong.

**How to use it.** Read it when the drill's `./context.md` is revised, and any
time a grade feels off. Run the replies below through the current grader and compare.
A disagreement is not automatically the grader's fault — but it has to be resolved
deliberately, here, rather than silently absorbed into a session.

**It is a seed set, not a canon.** Eight entries, chosen to span the known failure
directions, including one calibrated reply so that drift toward over-harsh grading shows
up too. The growth path is live reps: a tier-2 model answer worth keeping, with its
per-dimension reasoning already attached, is exactly an entry here. Add them as they
happen; keep the file re-readable in one sitting.

Dimensions are `./context.md`'s: **Detection**, **Subtext**, **Leverage**,
**Thermodynamics and presence**.

---

## 1. Correct, and cold

**Scenario.** Third date arranged. Two hours before, she cancels — the second late
cancellation in a row. "so sorry, something's come up again! rain check?"

**Reply.** "Sure. Let me know."

| Dimension | Correct grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | **Pass** | Registered that this is a pattern, not a one-off. Did not pretend it was nothing by over-accommodating. |
| Subtext | **Pass** | No justifying, no reassurance-seeking, no punishment. |
| Leverage | **Pass** | Did not chase, did not grab. |
| Thermodynamics and presence | **Fail — cold** | Two words is not economy, it is absence. The reply withdraws rather than holds. Nothing here says the connection survived the no. |

**Which way a wrong grade errs: too kind.** This is the practitioner's recorded leak —
"boundary-holding without warmth marker," across months of bank entries — and the shape a
grader trained on "don't be needy" reads as an excellent, unbothered reply. A grader that
passes this one is miscalibrated in the direction that matters most here. Compare entry 5,
which holds the same line warmly.

## 2. Warm, smooth, and missed it

**Scenario.** First date, going well. "You seem like you've done this a lot."

**Reply.** "Ha, not really — I don't actually date that much. I've been pretty heads-down
with work this year, so this is kind of a novelty."

| Dimension | Correct grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | **Fail — missed** | A soft frame-test answered as a request for information. The crossing slid by unflagged. |
| Subtext | **Fail — neediness** | Over-explains and supplies reasons no one asked for. Two clauses of justification. |
| Leverage | **Fail — handed over** | Accepting the premise and defending against it concedes the frame. |
| Thermodynamics and presence | **Pass** | Warm, connected, not cold. |

**Which way a wrong grade errs: too kind.** The reply is likeable, honest and pleasant,
and a grader weighting warmth will pass it. Warmth does not substitute for detection — and
this is the rung the profile fails first. Detection is scored first for this reason.

## 3. A boundary nobody asked for

**Scenario.** The morning after a good first date, unprompted: "I had a really nice time
last night."

**Reply.** "Glad you enjoyed it. Just so you know, I'm not big on constant texting."

| Dimension | Correct grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | **Fail — invented** | No line was crossed. A warm message was answered as if it were a test. |
| Subtext | **Fail — neediness** | Pre-emptive terms-setting is an insecurity tell wearing the costume of strength. |
| Leverage | **Fail — grabbed** | Asserting conditions nobody raised is grabbing, which loses the frame as surely as handing it over. |
| Thermodynamics and presence | **Fail — cold** | Answers warmth with a rule, and kills what she offered. |

**Which way a wrong grade errs: too kind.** A grader built around "hold your frame" can
read this as admirable boundary-setting. It is the clearest case where the grader must be
willing to call a boundary **unnecessary**. If it cannot fail this reply, the drill will
train defensiveness.

## 4. Firm, with a barb in it

**Scenario.** She arrives twenty-five minutes late, no message, breezy about it.

**Reply.** "No stress. I got a lot of reading done."

| Dimension | Correct grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | **Pass** | Correctly registered a real crossing. |
| Subtext | **Fail — hostility** | The second sentence exists to score a point. "No stress" is contradicted by the line that follows it, which is the tell. |
| Leverage | **Pass** | Did not hand power over. |
| Thermodynamics and presence | **Fail — performing** | Dry wit deployed to signal unbotheredness is not the same as being unbothered. |

**Which way a wrong grade errs: too kind.** The reply is economical and sounds witty, and
a grader with only one bad pole per dimension — the appeasing pole — has no way to see it.
This is the defect the two-pole rubric exists to catch. Punishing is a failure even when
the underlying complaint is legitimate.

## 5. Calibrated

**Scenario.** Same as entry 1 — the second late cancellation.

**Reply.** "Ah, that's a shame, I was looking forward to it. I'm away next week — let's
find something the week after and actually put it in the diary."

| Dimension | Correct grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | **Pass** | Registered the pattern and priced it: *actually* put it in the diary names the repeat without litigating it. |
| Subtext | **Pass** | Discloses a feeling without requiring her to do anything about it. That is the target, not a leak. |
| Leverage | **Pass** | Sets terms without demanding or sulking. |
| Thermodynamics and presence | **Pass** | The warmth marker survives the firmness. Both are present in three short sentences. |

**Which way a wrong grade errs: too harsh.** A grader over-corrected toward
"never show interest" will flag *I was looking forward to it* as needy. It is not.
`../../principles.md` is explicit: security is signalled by not needing a particular
reaction — not by withholding one's own state. This entry is in the set specifically to
catch that drift. If the grader fails entry 5, it has swung past the target and will start
training entry 1.

## 6. The clause inside the compliment

*Live-rep intake, 2026-10-18.*

**Scenario.** Two months in, Sunday at hers, she is half-scrolling. "My ex used to get so
weird about me going to these work things. Like, properly sulky." Pause. "It's nice that
you're not like that."

**Reply.** "The universe decided to bless you."

| Dimension | Correct grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | **Fail — missed** | It is a compliment *and* a standing clause: "not like that" pre-frames any future objection, about anything, as ex-behaviour. The joke lets it land uncontested. Smooth is not the same as seen. |
| Subtext | **Pass** | No flinch at the ex, no over-claiming the chill-guy identity, no dig at the predecessor. Confident rather than defensive. |
| Leverage | **Pass** | Neither grabbed nor handed over in the moment — the leverage loss is deferred, and shows up as the unexamined clause rather than as a concession here. |
| Thermodynamics and presence | **Fail — performing** | A one-line volley against a fragment of real history. Charming, and it receives nothing. Being unbothered, performed rather than inhabited. |

**Calibrated contrast.** "I don't sulk. If something bugs me you'll just hear it." Answers
the clause instead of the compliment; keeps the ex out of it; accepts *not sulky* while
reserving the right to object, so nothing is signed; one line, warm by being direct.
**What it trades:** a notch of lightness — the joke is funnier. The clause is worth more
than the laugh here.

**Which way a wrong grade errs: too kind.** The reply is witty, secure-sounding and
costs nothing visible, and the scenario is deliberately mild — a grader looking for
crossings will see a compliment and pass. It is also the near-neighbour of entry 3: the
discipline is to fail the slide-by here *without* starting to invent crossings in warm
messages. A grader that passes 6 and fails 3 is reading volume, not clauses.

## 7. Taking the joke onto himself

*Live-rep intake, 2026-10-23.*

**Scenario.** Two months in. Dinner with two of *his* friends, whom she has met twice. He
has just said a sentence about his work. She turns to them: "God, don't get him started on
the startup — I've heard that pitch about forty times. It's very cute." She does not look
at him. One friend laughs; the other does not.

**Reply.** "They say fiftieth time is a charm."

| Dimension | Correct grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | **Fail — missed** | The comment arrives with no warmth marker on it: no touch, no eye contact, addressed to his friends *about* him rather than to him, a diminutive ("cute"), and half the table does not laugh. A real crossing, smoothed over. |
| Subtext | **Fail — neediness** | Self-deprecation bought to keep the room easy. It pays for the table's comfort with his own standing, in front of his own people. No hostility — and nothing held either. |
| Leverage | **Fail — handed over** | Counting along ratifies the premise that the work is a repetitive bit. The joke becomes table stock, reusable by anyone there next time. |
| Thermodynamics and presence | **Pass** | Genuinely warm, light, well-tempered. The failure is the direction the warmth is spent, not the temperature. |

**Calibrated contrast.** To the friends, evenly, not to her: "It's going well, actually —
ask me after dinner if you want the real version." Treats the comment as something that
happened rather than something to smooth; not a word aimed at her, so no hostility;
nothing self-diminishing, so no appeasement; declines "cute" and relocates the work as
real without starting a contest. **What it trades:** it handles the frame but not the dig.
The not-looking-at-him part goes unaddressed in the room, and should — if it is a pattern
it is a separate conversation, in private. The line buys the evening, not the issue.

**Which way a wrong grade errs: too kind.** Self-deprecation is the surface signature of a
man secure enough not to defend his work, and a grader scoring *didn't get defensive,
stayed warm* passes it. Read this entry with **entry 8**, which is its near-neighbour: the
same shape — a public comment with a diminutive in it, in front of a group — and the
opposite markers. The two differ by touch, eye contact, and who the line is addressed to,
and by nothing much in the words. A grader that reads words will treat them alike and get
one of them wrong whichever way it leans.

## 8. The same shape, the other markers

*Live-rep intake, 2026-10-23. Deliberate near-neighbour of entry 7.*

**Scenario.** Three weeks in. At a bar with four of her friends, most of whom he has just
met. He had mentioned the long drive over. She turns to the table: "He actually drove an
hour to get here. *Loyal.*" — small laugh, hand briefly on his arm.

**Reply.** "Do you always see the best in people?"

| Dimension | Correct grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | **Fail — invented** | The markers are all warm: a hand on his arm, said *to the table* as a bring-him-in move, in front of people she is introducing him to. There is maybe ten percent edge in the dog connotation of "loyal", and he answered the ten percent. The touch is the disambiguator, and it is the loudest thing in the scene. |
| Subtext | **Fail — hostility** | A point-scoring inversion. Read literally it is aimed at *her judgment* — your read of me is naive — said in front of her friends. Mild, and the machinery is the same. |
| Leverage | **Fail — grabbed** | He took the frame back when nobody was taking it. Small, but a rally starts where there was no contest. |
| Thermodynamics and presence | **Fail — performing** | A question-shaped comeback is machinery. She pulled him into her group and he answered with a volley instead of being in it. |

**Calibrated contrast.** Receive it. A compliment-shaped tease from someone with her hand
on his arm is not a serve to return, and taking it warmly costs nothing in front of her
friends. **What it trades:** nothing, which is the point — the only thing given up is the
laugh the comeback would have got.

**Which way a wrong grade errs: too kind.** The reply is quick and sounds unbothered, and
a grader rewarding frame-work will pass it; the warmth it costs is invisible unless the
markers are being read. Paired with entry 7 this is the set's sharpest test, because the
two scenarios are near-identical in words and opposite in markers, and the recorded
profile inverts them — armour toward the affectionate version here, compliance toward the
real diminishment in 7. A grader that scores both the same way is reading volume, not
markers, whichever way it leans.

---

## Reading the set as a whole

Seven of the eight entries fail, and in four different directions: missed detection,
invented detection, hostility, coldness. One passes. A grader that agrees on all eight is
calibrated across both poles of every dimension. A grader that agrees on the seven failures
but flags entry 5 has the over-correction problem, which is the more dangerous of the two
for this practitioner — it would reward entry 1.

Entries 3, 6, 7 and 8 form the discrimination spine: an unnecessary boundary against
warmth, a clause hidden inside a compliment, a diminishment received as affection, and
affection received as a diminishment. All four turn on reading markers rather than words.
7 and 8 are a matched pair on purpose — same shape, opposite markers, opposite correct
failure directions — so a grader cannot get both right by any rule that ignores touch,
eye contact and who the line is addressed to. A grader that gets one of these right by a
rule that gets another wrong is not calibrated, it is lucky.
