# Rubric — Dating Frame-Control

How a response in this drill is graded. Drill-specific: a different boundary drill
(e.g. saying-no-at-work) would define its own dimensions. The cross-cutting capacities
(recognition, and calibration in *both* directions) live in `../../principles.md`; the
dimensions below operationalize them for dating pressure.

- **Detection** — did the response register that a line was actually crossed? A soft or
  ambiguous test that slides by unflagged is a miss, even when what was said sounds smooth.
  The opposite miss counts too: treating a warm or neutral message as a test is a
  detection failure in the other direction. Scored first on purpose — recognition is the
  rung that breaks first for this profile (`../../principles.md`), and nothing downstream
  fires without it.
- **Subtext** — does the response signal security, or does it leak *either* neediness
  (over-explaining, justifying) *or* hostility (punishing, point-scoring)? Both are off-target.
- **Leverage** — was power handed over, or grabbed at? Both lose the frame; holding it
  means neither.
- **Thermodynamics** — was the response clean and elegant, or bloated and reactive — or
  clipped and cold in a way that reads as withholding? Coldness is not economy.

After grading, the simulator reports how the other party would likely feel receiving the
reply, then escalates to the next scenario.
