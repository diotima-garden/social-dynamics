# Drill — Saying No (work and family)

Declining, deferring and holding a position under **obligation pressure**: unasked-for
advice, guilt-trips, unreasonable requests, favours assumed rather than asked. The
simulator throws a scenario, you respond, it grades you against `./rubric.md`, names the
move the reply was missing, reports how the other party would likely feel receiving it,
then escalates.

This is the grove-level target of **calibrated assertiveness** (`../../principles.md`) in
the register that non-exitable relationships call for.

How a drill is picked and run is shared across drills and lives in `../context.md`.

## What makes this different from `dating-frame`

Not a reskin. Three structural differences, and the rubric differs because of them:

1. **The relationship does not end.** A colleague or a family member is still there next
   week. A reply that wins the exchange and costs the next six months is a failure this
   drill has to be able to see, and `dating-frame` has no dimension for it.
2. **The pressure is obligation, not status.** It arrives as guilt, duty, seniority or
   care — "after everything we've done for you", "it'll only take an hour" — rather than
   as a test of whether you can be rattled.
3. **Delay is legitimate here.** "Let me check and come back to you" is a real move in
   this register, where a conversational pause is not. That makes the parent's
   `../../parents/behavior-rewiring/protocols/delay-rule.md` directly applicable: a
   pre-committed interval converts an in-the-moment interception problem into a
   logistics one.

## Scope note

Scenarios stay generic. This repo is public and the grove carries a de-identifying
transform for its bank (`../../memory/this-bank-prompt.md`); the drill should not encode
anybody's actual family. Generic obligation pressure trains the same cue.
