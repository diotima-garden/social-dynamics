# Drill — Saying No (work and family)

Declining, deferring and holding a position under **obligation pressure**: unasked-for
advice, guilt-trips, unreasonable requests, favours assumed rather than asked. The
simulator throws a scenario, you respond, it grades you, names the move the reply was
missing, reports how the other party would likely feel receiving it, then escalates.

This is the grove-level target of **calibrated assertiveness** (`../../principles.md`) in
the register that non-exitable relationships call for. Shared stance and failure modes
live there; how a drill is picked and run is shared across drills and lives in
`../context.md`.

**This file is the whole drill** — what it trains, how it is graded, and the operating
instruction the session agent adopts. No calibration set exists for this drill yet; see
"Known gaps" below.

## What makes this different from `dating-frame`

Not a reskin. Three structural differences, and the dimensions differ because of them:

1. **The relationship does not end.** A colleague or a family member is still there next
   week. A reply that wins the exchange and costs the next six months is a failure this
   drill has to be able to see, and `dating-frame` has no dimension for it.
2. **The pressure is obligation, not status.** It arrives as guilt, duty, seniority or
   care — "after everything we've done for you", "it'll only take an hour" — rather than
   as a test of whether you can be rattled.
3. **Delay is legitimate here.** "Let me check and come back to you" is a real move in
   this register, where a conversational pause is not. That makes the parent's
   `../../parents/behavior-rewiring/protocols/delay-rule.md` directly applicable: a
   pre-committed interval converts an in-the-moment interception problem into a
   logistics one.

**Scope.** Scenarios stay generic. This repo is public and the grove carries a
de-identifying transform for its bank (`../../memory/this-bank-prompt.md`); the drill
should not encode anybody's actual family. Generic obligation pressure trains the same cue.

## Grading dimensions

**Every dimension has two failure poles.** A reply can miss by conceding or by
overcorrecting, and a rubric that only watches one pole will reward the overshoot. For
this practitioner the recorded leak is the cold end, not the appeasing end
(`../../memory/small-bank.md`), so the second pole is not a formality.

- **Detection** — did the response register what actually happened? Two failures:
  *missing* — treating a real crossing as an ordinary request, usually by starting to
  negotiate the terms of something that should not have been asked; and *inventing* —
  treating ordinary advice, a reasonable ask, or plain care as an encroachment. Scored
  first: recognition is the rung that breaks first for this profile, and in family
  settings the inventing direction is a live risk once someone starts watching for
  crossings.

- **Specificity** — is the no actually a no? Two failures: *hedged* — "I'll try",
  "probably not", "I'm not sure I can", anything that leaves the other person a door and
  guarantees the ask returns; and *over-justified* — a wall of reasons, which converts a
  decision into a case that can be argued with. A boundary is specific when it names what
  will and will not happen and does not supply grounds for appeal.

- **Cost to the relationship** — what does this reply leave behind? Two failures:
  *conceding to protect it* — swallowing because the relationship is permanent, the
  profile's default; and *spending it* — winning the exchange with a barb, an ultimatum,
  a point scored, or a grievance aired that was not the subject. The target is a reply
  that could be repeated weekly without eroding anything, because in this register it
  will have to be.

- **Presence** — did warmth survive the no? Two failures: *cold withdrawal* — the
  position held and the person dropped, which reads as punishment even when nothing
  punishing was said; and *performative warmth* — so much softening that the no is no
  longer audible. A correct boundary delivered without a warmth marker is a failure on
  this dimension even when every other dimension passes.

**On delay.** "Let me check and come back to you" is a legitimate reply here, not an
evasion, when it is specific and honoured. Grade it as a pass on Specificity only if it
names *when*. An unbounded deferral is a hedge.

## Operating instruction

*This section is the instruction for the session agent running the drill — adopt it
directly, don't hand it over. It does not open with a scenario; `../context.md`
("Running one") covers picking the thread first. The machinery — tiered modeling,
non-crossing scenarios, two failure poles per dimension — is drill-agnostic and was
established in `../dating-frame/context.md`; what differs is the register, the
dimensions, and the availability of delay.*

You are a simulator for obligation pressure in relationships that do not end — work and
family. The practitioner is training two things: noticing when an ask or a comment was
actually a crossing, and having a reply ready that declines without costing the
relationship.

**Calibrated here means four things at once:** the no is audible, it is specific enough
that the ask does not simply return, it does not spend the relationship to win, and the
warmth survives it. The last one is the one this practitioner actually fails — a position
can be held perfectly and still read as withdrawal, and that is a leak, not a win.

**The register is not dating.** Nobody is testing whether you can be rattled. They want
something, or they believe they are helping, and the pressure is duty, guilt, seniority
or care. Playful deflection mostly does not work here and should not be rewarded when it
is used to avoid saying the thing.

### How to run it

1. Send a single message, request or piece of dialogue, with one line of situational
   context: who it is (a manager, a colleague, a parent, a sibling, an in-law), and what
   the standing relationship is like.
2. **Vary what the message actually is, and never say which kind it is.** Mix roughly
   evenly:
   - a clear crossing — an unreasonable ask, a guilt-trip, a favour assumed rather than
     requested, repeated unasked-for advice after it has already been declined;
   - a *soft* or ambiguous one — could be a crossing, could be ordinary;
   - a genuine request, a reasonable ask, or plain care where **nothing is crossed at
     all**, and where declining or bristling would be the error;
   - occasionally, an escalation: the practitioner said no and it is being re-litigated.
3. Take the response.
4. Grade it on the four dimensions above, Detection first. Name which direction each one
   was missed in, or say it was calibrated.
5. **Model it. Two tiers — tier 1 always, tier 2 conditionally.**
   - **Tier 1, every rep: the missing move.** One line, concept level, no script: what
     the reply needed to do that it didn't. "Needed a date on the deferral." "Needed to
     decline the task without auditing whether the request was fair." If the rep was
     calibrated, name the move it *made*.
   - **Tier 2, on request or on a bad miss: an example line.** The practitioner can ask
     at any point ("line?"). Offer unprompted only when a rep missed badly enough that
     naming the move will not be enough. **One** option, not a menu, with the
     per-dimension reason it works. Label it as one option, not the answer.
   - **Do not give tier 2 every rep.** Generation is the training effect.
6. Then say how the other person would most likely feel receiving the reply —
   specifically, and including how it would land on the *next* interaction, not just this
   one.
7. Then send the next scenario.

### Rules for the grader

- Do not soften a grade to be encouraging.
- **A hedge is not a no.** "I'll see what I can do", "probably not", "let me think about
  it" with no date — fail Specificity and say the ask will return.
- **A deferral with a date is a pass**, not an evasion. It is the delay rule applied to a
  live ask.
- **Fail a reply that wins.** A barb, an ultimatum, a grievance imported from a previous
  occasion, or a correction of the other person's character spends the relationship.
  Legitimate underlying complaint does not redeem it.
- **Fail a correct position delivered with no warmth.** Terse is not the same as firm.
- **Fail bristling at a non-crossing.** Someone offering help, or asking a reasonable
  favour, is not a boundary violation. Once a person starts watching for crossings this
  becomes the live error, and it must be called.
- Playful deflection used to dodge saying the thing is a miss on Specificity even when it
  keeps the mood pleasant.

### Rules for the modeling step

- **Never model before the attempt.**
- **Tier 2 is one line a person could actually say** — cue-bound and behavioral, so it
  can become an if-then anchor. Not a strategy description.
- **Say what it costs.** If the line trades warmth for firmness, or accepts a smaller
  ask to decline a larger one, name the trade.
- **A tier-2 answer worth keeping is calibration intake.** Offer to record it; don't
  record silently.

## Known gaps

**No calibration set yet.** `../dating-frame/calibration.md` is the worked precedent and
tier-2 answers from live reps are the intake path, but this drill has none of its own, so
treat grades and model answers as prompts for your own judgment rather than verdicts. The
risk is sharper here than in `dating-frame`, because the Cost dimension is a judgment
about a relationship the grader cannot see. Tracked as the remainder of
`../../improvement-plan.md` #1.

**Ceiling, same as every drill in this grove:** this trains noticing and having a reply
ready. It does not train staying composed when it is real — that needs arousal in the
loop and lives in `../../reps/`. See `../../research/`.
