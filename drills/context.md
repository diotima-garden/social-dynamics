# Drills — how to run one

One subfolder per drill, each self-contained: its own index, its own grading rubric, and
its own versioned simulator artifacts. This file is the part that is the same for all of
them. Read it before running a drill; read the drill's own `context.md` for what that drill
trains.

**Drills available:**

| Drill | Register | When to pick it |
|---|---|---|
| `dating-frame` | Status pressure — probing questions, shit-tests, late cancellations, vulnerability checks. | The relationship is new or exitable and the pressure is a test. |
| `saying-no` | Obligation pressure at work and in the family — guilt-trips, unasked-for advice, assumed favours. | The relationship does not end, and the cost of the reply outlives the exchange. |

Offer both when the practitioner hasn't said which. They share `../principles.md` and this
procedure; they have different rubrics because the registers differ.

## Running one

"Continue with the drills", or anything like it, is the request this procedure answers. Do
not ask the practitioner to supply a prompt, and do not invent a drill.

1. **Build the menu from the open threads the bank already shows** — patterns it flags for
   continued drilling, scenarios it records as unfinished. That is what "continue" refers
   to. Do not hardcode a list here; it would go stale against the bank. If the bank's recent
   entries are already in context, use them rather than re-reading
   `../memory/small-bank.md`.

   With one drill, the menu is over *resume points*, not over drills: continue the recurring
   pattern, pick up an unfinished scenario, or start a fresh set.

2. **Read the drill's own files — these are not auto-injected.**
   `./<name>/context.md`, `./<name>/rubric.md`, and `./<name>/artifacts/simulator.md`.
   That last path is always the live one; the simulator is the operating instruction, so
   adopt it rather than handing it over. Anything else in `artifacts/` is a superseded
   revision kept for comparison — never offer a choice of version, and don't mention that
   versions exist.

3. **Run it here.** You are the simulator. Grade against every rubric dimension, in both
   failure directions.

4. **Don't hand-write the bank at the end.** Capture is automatic and runs through
   `../memory/this-bank-prompt.md`'s de-identifying transform — abstract training signal
   only, nothing personal. A manual entry alongside an automatic one corrupts the record.

## Weight the bank over a fresh reading of `../principles.md`

The leak the bank currently reports is not the one the principles would predict, and a
session that drills the predicted failure instead of the recorded one trains the wrong
thing. The principles say what good looks like; the bank says which part of it *this*
practitioner keeps missing. Build scenarios against the second.

## The ceiling, state it when it matters

A text drill trains **recognition** and **having a reply ready** — rungs 1–2 of
`../principles.md`. It does not train staying composed when it is real; that is rung 3, it
needs arousal in the loop, and it lives in `../reps/`. Don't let a drill imply otherwise,
and don't put a rung-3 target on the menu. See `../research/` for why.
