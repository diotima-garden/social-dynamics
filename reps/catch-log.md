# Catch-Log — Sprint 1

An instantiation of `parents/behavior-rewiring/protocols/single-target-sprint.md` for this
grove's cue. The protocol is the parent's; the cue, the capture design and every line in
it are ours.

## The target

> **When** a conversation is over, **I** write one line: how many times a line was pushed
> on, and what is still sitting with me.

Then, weekly, with distance: **how many of those did I decline because it wasn't worth
making an issue of, and how many did I swallow?**

**Why not recognition-only.** This file originally ran a recognition-only sprint — notice
the crossing, change nothing — on the parent's advice that it is usually the right first
one. First contact with the practitioner retired that: detection is already online. The
report is 3–5 argument-baits per conversation, reliably noticed, reliably declined, plus
irritations correctly identified as his own and let go (the canonical example: being
annoyed at how someone cuts their food, noticing it, moving attention elsewhere — no line
was crossed, letting it go is correct). A recognition sprint would train an already
trained capacity.

**What is actually unknown, and therefore the target.** Whether the quiet-keeping is
*health* or *avoidance*. From outside, and often from inside, declining to make an issue
of something and swallowing it look identical. This grove's `principles.md` names both
failure directions — swallowing and attacking — and the parent's `open-questions.md` §4
warns that the introspective markers for this flatter the practitioner. So the sprint does
not ask for that judgment in the moment. It asks for a count in the moment and the sort
afterwards.

**The discriminator: residue.** Not "did it feel effortful" but **is it still bugging me
hours later.** Declining costs nothing and leaves nothing. Swallowing leaves residue. That
test is reportable without requiring honesty under pressure, which is why it is the one
being used.

- **Window:** 30 days from first entry.
- **One active target.** Everything else goes on the queue below.
- **Do not add fields.** The log competes with the thing it measures.
- **Proposed, not agreed.** Replace the cue if a different one fits better. Do not run two.

## Capture — where each part lives

The previous version of this file assumed a git repo was a workable capture surface
during normal life. It isn't, and a log that doesn't get filled is not a weaker version of
this protocol — it is the absence of it. Three surfaces, each doing the thing it is
actually good at:

| When | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| **During** the conversation | nowhere | Nothing. No phone, no note. Capture in the moment would cost the attention the thing itself needs. |
| **Right after** it ends | Google Keep, one pinned note | One line, appended. A count and a sentence. Fifteen seconds. |
| **Weekly**, on opening the paper journal | the journal | Read the week's Keep lines, sort them: declined vs swallowed. Write the two numbers and anything the sort revealed. |
| **At the 30-day ruling** | this file | Roll the journal's weekly numbers in and write the ruling. Not before. |

**Why the journal and not just the app.** Recording progress has its own effect on
attainment, and it is larger when the record is *physical* rather than a mental or purely
digital tally (`parents/behavior-rewiring/sources.md` §8). Keep is a buffer so nothing is
lost; the journal is where it counts as recorded.

**Why "when I open the journal" and not "Sundays."** The best-evidenced lever in the
parent node is the cue-bound if-then plan — a trigger that already occurs beats a date in
a calendar. The journal habit exists; this attaches to it rather than inventing a new one.

## The Keep line

One line, free text. Not a table, not fields to fill:

> `4 today — went quiet when he pushed on the job thing, still bugging me. Rest were nothing.`

A bare count is not enough. **The sentence is what makes the weekly sort possible** — in
seven days the numbers will all look alike and only the sentence will say which ones had
residue.
