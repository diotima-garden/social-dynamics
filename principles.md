# Principles — Social Dynamics

The durable stance every drill in this grove trains toward. Drill-agnostic; each drill's
concrete grading rubric lives in its own folder (`drills/<name>/rubric.md`) and expresses
this stance in that drill's own idiom (e.g. dating-frame renders it as "hold a playful
frame, don't leak neediness").

**Scope.** This file holds *what good looks like in social pressure* — the target and the
failure modes. It deliberately holds no theory of behavior change: how an understood
intention becomes an automatic one, how many changes can run at once, and what the
evidence grades are all live one layer up in
`parents/behavior-rewiring/principles.md`. Read that before designing a drill or a rep
loop; don't re-derive it here.

## The target: calibrated assertiveness
The goal is not "hold frame" and not "be tougher" — it is **calibration**: advocating for
yourself without either *swallowing* the crossing or *attacking* the other person. Two
failure directions, not one. A reformed conflict-avoider predictably overshoots from
appeasing into hostility, and both directions miss. Security is signaled by *not* needing a
particular reaction from the other person — held without punishing them for the test.

Three capacities underlie it, listed **in the order they tend to break for someone who
habitually swallows boundary-crossings** — that ordering is this grove's own claim about
this profile, and it is what drill design here should follow:

1. **Recognition** — noticing, in the moment, that a line was actually crossed. The first
   bottleneck for the appeaser, and the thing a low-stakes drill is genuinely good at
   building. If you never register the crossing, nothing downstream fires.
2. **Response** — having a calibrated reply available: neither over-explaining nor punishing.
3. **Regulation** — staying composed while the other person pushes back. Hardest to train in
   text (no real arousal) and the seam where real-world reps matter — see `research/`.

Rungs 1–2 are an *acquisition* problem and rung 3 a *performance* problem; that split, and
why a stakes-free drill addresses the first cleanly and the second barely, is the parent's
`principles.md` §5. The grove-specific part is the **order**: for this profile recognition
breaks first, which is why it is scored before fluency.

## Failure modes to catch — both directions

**Under-assertion (swallowing):**
- **Missing the crossing** — not registering that a boundary was breached at all.
- **Over-explaining** — supplying reasons no one asked for.
- **Justifying** — defending yourself against a test instead of holding your position.
- **Neediness** — handing your emotional state to the other person to manage.
- **Reactivity** — matching their energy instead of setting the temperature.

**Over-assertion (attacking):**
- **Hostility** — punishing the other person instead of simply stating the boundary.
- **Contempt / point-scoring** — winning the exchange at the cost of the relationship.
- **Rigidity** — refusing any negotiation once challenged, treating a request as an assault.
